    \section{Preliminaries}

    \begin{definition}
      A finite lattice $\L$ is \defn{max-flag} if its \defn{max-complex} $$\maxcomplex(\L):=\{\max \{j\in \JIrr(\L):j\le x\}:x\in \L\}$$ is a simplicial complex which is flag.
    \end{definition}
    
	\section{Max flag Permutrees}

    We can characterize permutree quotients which are max-flag.

    \begin{proposition}
        Let $\delta\in \{\decoX, \decoY, \decoL, \decoI\}^n$. The $\delta$-permutree quotient of the weak order on $\Sn$ is max-flag if and only if $\delta$ does not contain $\decoI\decoI$, $\decoI\decoL$, $\decoY\decoI$ or $\decoY\decoL$ consecutively.
    \end{proposition}
	
	On $\Sn$, there are $4\cdot 3^{n-3}$ permutree quotients which are max flag, out of which $2^{n-3}$ are minimal when ordering quotients by the inclusion of their forcing order filter.
    One can construct an automaton from binary words of size $n-3$ to minimal max flag permutree decorations in $\{\decoI, \decoL, \decoY,\decoX\}^{n-2}$.
	
	The patterns to be avoided are obtained from the following observations. In the weak order, the interval between the identity permutation and permutations of the form $(i,i+3)$ is not max flag. Thus, for each consecutive pair of decorations $\decoStar_i,\decoStar_{i+1}$ in a max flag permutree, at least one of $\decoL_i$ or $\decoY_{i+1}$.
	
	\begin{question}[Max flag permutrees]

			\item Combinatorial description of compatibility relations in the Max Complex.

	\end{question}
